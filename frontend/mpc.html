What the plan was trying to achieve

      

        Every day the system balances four competing goals. The wider a band, the more that
        goal influenced the release decision for this date.
      

      


      
Why these priorities

      


      
Was the plan safe to apply?

      


      

        How the plan is chosen (technical detail)
        

          

            For each day the optimiser (NSGA-II) produces a Pareto front - many
            release plans, none strictly better than the others, each trading the four goals
            off differently. Something must pick one.
          

          

            That choice is made by TOPSIS (Technique for Order Preference by
            Similarity to Ideal Solution). It scores every candidate plan by how close it sits
            to the theoretical best on all four goals at once, and how far it sits from the
            theoretical worst. The plan with the best ratio wins.
          

          

            The four goals are weighted, and the weights are not fixed - they shift with
            Module 3's risk forecast. High drought risk raises shortage and
            equity; high overflow risk raises overflow and lowers
            loss. Every weight is floored so no goal is ever ignored entirely,
            then renormalised to sum to 1. That is the "Why these priorities" section above.
          

          

            Feasible means the winning plan satisfied every physical
            constraint: storage stayed within each tank's safe range (C1), releases stayed
            within valve limits (C2), and the seasonal reserve floor held (C3).
